# Vertical Software for Service, Storefront and Practice-Based Small Businesses (as of Oct 2026)

Method note: Built from 17 web searches (Oct 29, 2026). Many available sources are vendor blogs or affiliate comparison pages; those are flagged. Items marked **[background, not verified this session]** come from the researcher's prior knowledge and were not confirmed in a source this session. The report writer should treat them as context, not as cited fact.

## Q1. What are the dominant tools and legacy holdouts per vertical, what workflows do they cover, and do they have APIs/integrations?

### Takeaway
Every vertical has a clear modern cloud leader with a spreadsheet of add-ons, plus a legacy on-prem holdout. Incumbents are now shipping AI agents themselves: ServiceTitan Atlas/Max, Jobber Receptionist, Housecall Pro AI Team, Toast IQ, Dentrix Ascend Eligibility Pro, Weave insurance capture, and Jane AI Scribe. That shrinks the room for standalone point tools and raises the value of integration. API access ranges from open (Shopify, Square, Clover, Open Dental) to gated (Airbnb, many EHRs and PMSs).

### Inferences
- The "AI-native" layer in most verticals is landing in two places. (a) Incumbents bundle AI receptionists, scribes and agents (ServiceTitan, Jobber, HCP, Toast, Jane, Dentrix Ascend, Weave). (b) Horizontal AI voice/text startups integrate into the incumbent's calendar or POS. Independent tools win only where they write back into the system of record.
- API openness is a strong predictor of how automatable a vertical is. It is highest in retail and restaurant POS (Shopify, Square, Clover) and modern cloud practice tools (Open Dental, Jane, Jobber). It is lowest in legacy on-prem PMS/POS (Cornerstone, Dentrix G-series, Mitchell 1 Manager SE, Aloha on-prem) and gated platforms (Airbnb, Reynolds DMS). In those, automation often falls back to RPA, browser agents or CSV exports.
- Discontinued or retired products to flag: QuickBooks Desktop POS (Oct 2023), the Vend brand (now Lightspeed X-Series), Kareo and PatientPop brands (now Tebra; background), and HiMama (now Lillio; background).

## Q2. What manual/repetitive tasks are most common and automation-ripe?

### Takeaway
Across verticals, the most automation-ripe tasks are: answering the phone and booking (missed calls), reminders and no-show handling with deposits, quoting and estimate follow-up, review requests, rebooking/recall, insurance eligibility verification (dental and medical), clinical documentation (SOAP notes), invoice/AP and food-cost entry (restaurants), and intake forms and records (vaccines, waivers, childcare enrollment). Vendors are building into exactly these slots.

### Inferences
- Per-vertical "top manual tasks" checklist (synthesis of the above plus background knowledge; the report writer should present it as synthesis):
  - Home services: call answering and booking, quote/estimate follow-up, dispatch changes, membership/maintenance agreement renewals, invoice chasing, review requests, permit paperwork, and converting paper work orders.
  - Restaurants: phone orders and reservations, scheduling and shift swaps, invoice entry and food costing, delivery tablet consolidation and menu sync across DoorDash/Uber Eats, review responses, and inventory counts and reorder.
  - Retail: inventory reorder and purchase orders, catalog and product data entry, syncing in-store with e-commerce, vendor invoice matching, and loyalty/marketing emails.
  - Salons and spas: booking by DM, text and phone, reminders and confirmations, deposits and no-show fees, rebooking, waitlist fill, review requests, and product reorder.
  - Fitness: lead follow-up, trial-to-member conversion, failed-payment dunning, cancellations and freezes, and waiver collection.
  - Medical, dental and therapy: intake forms, insurance eligibility and benefits, prior authorizations, recall/reactivation, claims and EOB posting, AR follow-up, and documentation.
  - Veterinary: reminders for vaccines and wellness, SOAP notes, inventory of pharmaceuticals, and callbacks.
  - Auto: estimates and authorization texts, parts ordering and price comparison, DVI photo reports, and service reminders.
  - Childcare: enrollment paperwork, immunization and health form tracking, subsidy billing, ratio and attendance logs, and parent messaging and daily reports.
  - Pet: vaccine record verification (owners upload vet PDFs), waivers, capacity and kennel assignment, and reminders.
  - Hospitality: guest messaging, check-in instructions and door codes, cleaning turnover scheduling, dynamic pricing, review responses, and owner statements.
- Vendor-claimed ROI numbers dominate this space. There is little independent evidence on outcomes such as no-show reduction or conversion lift.

## Q3. What compliance constraints affect automation (HIPAA, PCI, childcare regulations)?

### Takeaway
HIPAA (BAAs, PHI handling, and AI training/retention terms) is the gating constraint for medical, dental and therapy automation; vet records are generally outside HIPAA (background). PCI DSS v4.0.1 is fully in force since March 31, 2025, and pushes small merchants toward hosted and tokenized payments, which means automation should never touch raw card data. Childcare automation is bounded by state-by-state licensing rules (ratios, records), which software makes the center configure itself.

### Cited Findings
- Jane signs BAAs for HIPAA-covered subscribers but does not publish its BAA text, so practices should request it — [Paubox](https://www.paubox.com/blog/is-jane-app-hipaa-compliant-2025-update). Jane's AI Scribe claims HIPAA/PIPEDA compliance and no training on customer data — [Jane](https://jane.app/features/charting-ai-scribe)
- SimplePractice claims HIPAA compliance and HITRUST certification. Whether its AI note feature is covered by the BAA was not confirmed — [SimplePractice](https://simplepractice.com/compare/simplepractice-vs-jane-app)
- Housecall Pro AI Team reportedly supports HIPAA via a signed BAA on eligible plans (third-party claim) — [Stork](https://www.stork.ai/compare/housecall-pro-ai-team-vs-jobber-ai-receptionist)
- Standalone AI scribes advertise BAAs (vendor claim) — [S10.ai](https://s10.ai/blog/ai-medical-scribe-progress-notetaker-for-jane)

### Inferences
- Automation design rule for an intake/automation offering: route payments through processor-hosted links or tokenized POS APIs (Square, Toast, Clover, Stripe) rather than having an agent capture card numbers by voice or text. Doing so keeps the business in a lighter SAQ.
- For healthcare verticals, any automation vendor touching PHI (call transcripts, intake forms, eligibility data) needs a BAA. LLM providers in the chain must also be covered (BAA or zero-retention), and practices should check AI-feature-specific BAA coverage, not just the core EHR's.
- [background, not verified this session] Other constraints: 42 CFR Part 2 (substance-use records) for some therapists. State veterinary records laws (vet data is not HIPAA). FERPA does not generally apply to private childcare or tutoring, but COPPA applies to apps collecting data from children under 13. Childcare also has immunization and health-record retention and state subsidy (CCAP) attendance documentation rules. The TCPA/10DLC registration and consent rules govern automated SMS reminders and marketing in every vertical. Restaurants face state tip/scheduling laws (e.g., predictive scheduling in NYC, SF, Seattle, Chicago, Oregon) that affect automated shift changes. Auto repair has state written-estimate/authorization laws (e.g., California BAR). Short-term rentals have local STR permit, registration and occupancy-tax rules.
