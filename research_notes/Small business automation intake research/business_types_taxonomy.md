# Small Business Types: Landscape, Classification, and an Intake-Form Taxonomy

Research note (Oct 29, 2026). Method caveat: in this session WebFetch could not reach census.gov, advocacy.sba.gov, or uschamber.com because those hosts are outside the allowed network domains. Findings below come from search-result summaries of those primary pages plus secondary sources. Figures labelled "secondary" should be checked against the primary tables (Census SUSB 2022, NES 2022, BTOS) before they are published.

## 1. How many small businesses exist, and how are they distributed by industry?

### Takeaway
The US has about 36.2 million small businesses (SBA Advocacy, June 2025 profiles). Roughly 30 million of them are nonemployers (solo operators) and about 6 million are employer firms, and about 89% of the employer firms have fewer than 20 employees. Among nonemployers, the largest NAICS sectors by count are professional/technical services, transportation and warehousing (largely gig and delivery drivers), real estate, and construction. For an automation app, this means the typical user is a solo operator or a micro-team.

### Cited Findings
**US totals**
- 2025 SBA Advocacy profiles (released June 30, 2025): **36.2 million small businesses**, accounting for almost 46% of private-sector employment. Small businesses created about 9 of every 10 net new jobs from March 2023 to March 2024. California had the most, with 4.34 million — [SBA Advocacy press release](https://advocacy.sba.gov/?p=30239); [state small business statistics 2025 (data.sba.gov)](https://data.sba.gov/dataset/state-small-business-statistics-2025)
- The earlier 2024 profiles (Nov 19, 2024) reported 34.8 million small businesses and 45.9% of employment — [SBA Advocacy](https://advocacy.sba.gov/2024/11/19/new-advocacy-report-shows-small-business-total-reaches-34-8-million-accounting-for-2-6-million-net-new-jobs-in-latest-year-of-data/)
- SBA Advocacy published "Frequently Asked Questions About Small Business 2026" on Feb 3, 2026. Its industry section could not be retrieved — [SBA Advocacy FAQ category](https://advocacy.sba.gov/category/research/frequently-asked-questions)

**Nonemployers (Census Nonemployer Statistics, 2022 reference year, released Dec 12, 2024)**
- **29.8 million (29,811,495) nonemployer businesses** with combined receipts of $1.7 trillion, about 6.8% of the 2022 economy. A nonemployer has no paid employees and at least $1,000 in receipts ($1 in construction) — [Census story, May 2025](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2025/05/smallest-businesses.html); [Census 2022 NES tip sheet](https://census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024/2022-nonemployer-statistics.html)
- Largest sectors by count, 2022:
  - Professional, Scientific and Technical Services: 4,013,209 (13.5% of nonemployers; $229.4B receipts, 13.3%)
  - Transportation and Warehousing: 3,854,720
  - Real Estate, Rental and Leasing: 3,145,367 (10.6% of establishments but 20.0% of receipts, $344.7B)
  - Construction: 9.6% of nonemployer establishments

  Source: [Census story](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2025/05/smallest-businesses.html)
- The 2022 NES release changed its methodology, so comparisons with earlier years need care. Data covers 18 NAICS sectors — [Census NES API page](https://www.census.gov/data/developers/data-sets/nonemp-api.2022.html)
- Nonemployer counts grew faster than employer counts in nearly every year from 2012 to 2023 — [Census story, Jul 2025](https://census.gov/library/stories/2025/07/nonemployer-business-growth.html)
- A secondary site reports 2024 NES figures of 4,233,838 professional services and 3,263,571 real estate nonemployers. These are unverified — [onemanonly.com](https://onemanonly.com/posts/industries-most-nonemployer-businesses)

**Employer firms and size bands**
- About 6.4 million employer firms in 2022, per secondary analyses of Census data (SBE Council; Review42 gives 6,395,635) — [SBE Council](https://sbecouncil.org/about-us/facts-and-data/); [Review42](https://resources.review42.com/small-business-statistics/)
- Census Business Dynamics Statistics / SUSB: 5.52 million firms with 1–499 employees in 2022 — [Kaplan Group citing SUSB](https://www.kaplancollectionagency.com/business-advice/54-small-business-statistics-for-2025/)
- Firms with fewer than 20 employees are about 89% of employer firms — [SBE Council](https://sbecouncil.org/about-us/facts-and-data/)
- Among roughly 6 million small employer businesses (ABS estimates, 2021 data), 49% have 1–4 workers and 27% have 5–19 — [Pew Research, Apr 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/04/22/a-look-at-small-businesses-in-the-us/)
- Construction example (secondary): about 719,000 employer firms with 1–19 employees versus about 62,000 with 20–499 — [Review42](https://resources.review42.com/small-business-statistics/)
- Official employer counts by industry and size are in the 2022 SUSB annual tables (NAICS sectors 23, 62, 72, 81, and so on) — [Census SUSB 2022 tables](https://www.census.gov/data/tables/2022/econ/susb/2022-susb-annual.html)

**International**
- **UK:** about 5.69 million private-sector businesses at the start of 2025, of which about 5.68 million (99.85%) are SMEs. About 4.27 million (around 75%) have no employees. SMEs account for about 16.9 million jobs. Only about 2.7 million firms are VAT/PAYE-registered — [Deplexifi summarizing DBT BPE 2025](https://deplexifi.com/uk/insights/small-business-statistics-uk); [gov.uk BPE 2024](https://gov.uk/government/statistics/business-population-estimates-2024/business-population-estimates-for-the-uk-and-regions-2024-statistical-release)
- **Canada:** 1.10 million employer businesses as of Dec 2024, of which 1.08 million (98.2%) are small (1–99 employees) — [ISED Key Small Business Statistics 2025](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/sme-research-statistics/en/key-small-business-statistics/key-small-business-statistics-2025). Statistics Canada location-based counts, reported by a secondary source: 1.37M employer and 3.67M nonemployer businesses (Dec 2025) — [Whizi](https://whizi.io/resources/small-business-statistics-canada/)
- **Australia:** 2,729,648 actively trading businesses in 2024-25. About 64% are non-employing — [ASBFEO citing ABS](https://www.asbfeo.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-12/Number%20of%20small%20businesses%20in%20Australia_Aug%202025.pdf)
- **India:** about 7.3 crore (73 million) MSMEs registered on the Udyam portal and Udyam Assist Platform (Jul 2020 to Dec 17, 2025). Udyam Assist accounts for 2.92 crore of these and targets informal micro enterprises — [MSME Year-End Review 2025](https://www.aviation-defence-universe.com/year-end-review-2025-ministry-of-micro-small-medium-enterprises/); [Lok Sabha answer: 7.22 crore as of Nov 30, 2025](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/3017323/1/AU1883_goP9ZQ.pdf)

### Inferences
- Roughly 80% of US small businesses are nonemployers, so the default user is a solo operator. The form should ask about team size early, and the generated plans should work for one person with no IT staff.
- Transportation and warehousing ranks second among nonemployers mainly because of rideshare and delivery gig workers. They have little to automate in their own operations, so the form should not give them a top-level category. A "Trucking / courier / moving" sub-type is enough.
- Real estate's large share of receipts and professional services' large share of counts make both high-value segments for the app.

### Gaps
- Official 2022 SUSB employer firm counts by NAICS sector for firms under 20 and under 500 employees could not be retrieved because census.gov was blocked. The product team should pull this table before quoting sector shares of employer firms.
- I could not get a full 2022 NES sector ranking beyond the top three plus construction (for example "other services" and health care counts).
- Revenue-band distribution: I found no retrieved source. SUSB has receipts-size tables.
- The micro-enterprise share of India's Udyam registrations was not found.

## 2. Most common small business verticals

### Takeaway
NAICS sectors are too coarse for an intake form. "Other Services" (NAICS 81), for example, combines auto repair, salons, dry cleaning, pet care and religious organizations. The verticals that recur in counts and in vertical-SaaS markets are:
- home and trade services (construction, plumbing, HVAC, electrical, cleaning, landscaping)
- professional services (accounting, legal, consulting, marketing/design, IT)
- real estate
- health and wellness practices
- personal care (beauty)
- food service
- retail and e-commerce
- transportation
- fitness
- education and childcare
- pet care
- auto services
- makers and light manufacturing
- nonprofits

### Cited Findings
- The top nonemployer sectors by count are professional/scientific/technical services, transportation and warehousing, and real estate. Construction is about 9.6% — [Census story](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2025/05/smallest-businesses.html)
- Construction has about 719K employer firms with 1–19 employees, which makes it one of the largest employer verticals (secondary) — [Review42](https://resources.review42.com/small-business-statistics/)
- NAICS sector codes used by SUSB include 23 Construction, 62 Health Care and Social Assistance, 72 Accommodation and Food Services, and 81 Other Services. SUSB 2022 is coded on 2017 NAICS — [Census SUSB tables](https://www.census.gov/data/tables/2022/econ/susb/2022-susb-annual.html)
- The US Chamber's 2025 sector chart covers technology, financial services, entertainment/media, construction, agriculture, and restaurant/dining, among others — [US Chamber 2025 report](https://www.uschamber.com/assets/documents/Empowering-Small-Business-Report-2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- The form needs to map plain-language verticals to NAICS codes, not show NAICS labels. Each sub-type can store a NAICS code in the background for later benchmarking against Census data.
- "Other Services (81)" should be split into separate top-level choices: Beauty & Personal Care, Auto Services, Pet Services, and Repair. Each has a distinct workflow, such as appointments, job tickets, or boarding.

### Gaps
- No single authoritative ranking of fine-grained verticals (for example "number of US hair salons" or "number of plumbing businesses") was retrieved. These are available from County Business Patterns and NES at the 6-digit NAICS level.

## 3. Operating-model dimensions that matter for automation

### Takeaway
Industry alone does not predict which automations fit. Two businesses in the same vertical can need different tools; a mobile groomer and a grooming salon are an example. Ask about the dimensions below separately from industry.

### Cited Findings
- Size matters for adoption: 51% of firms with 1–9 employees introduced new or significantly improved technology in the last two years, compared with 75% of firms with 50+ employees. The overall figure is 57%. 22% adopted only software and 22% only equipment — [NFIB Small Business and Technology 2025 (survey of 20,000 members, Mar 6–31, 2025)](https://www.nfib.com/news/press-release/new-nfib-report-how-small-businesses-incorporate-tech-and-ai-advancements/); [Small Biz Trends summary](https://smallbiztrends.com/new-nfib-report-reveals-how-small-businesses-harness-tech-and-ai/)
- Size bands in official data: nonemployer, 1–4, 5–19, 20–99, 100–499 (Census/Pew). The UK uses 0 / 1–9 / 10–49 / 50–249. Canada defines small as 1–99 — [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/04/22/a-look-at-small-businesses-in-the-us/); [Deplexifi/DBT](https://deplexifi.com/uk/insights/small-business-statistics-uk); [ISED](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/sme-research-statistics/en/key-small-business-statistics/key-small-business-statistics-2025)
- Census found that AI use rises with firm size, but generative AI may have an outsized effect on small firms. A Fed note (Apr 2026) found adoption among the smallest firms is stronger than size alone would predict — [Census blog, Dec 2024](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/blogs/research-matters/2024/12/ai-use-small-businesses.html?tp=1); [Federal Reserve FEDS Note, Apr 3, 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/monitoring-ai-adoption-in-the-u-s-economy-20260403.html)

### Inferences (proposed form dimensions; analytical, not sourced)
1. **Revenue model and how work arrives.** Options: appointment/booking; walk-in or order-at-counter; project/quote-to-job (estimates, change orders, milestones); recurring service or subscription (cleaning routes, retainers, memberships); product sales (inventory, shipping); or a mix. This drives the core automation stack:
   - Appointment-based: booking, reminders, no-show reduction
   - Project-based: lead intake, quote, invoice, deposits
   - Subscription: recurring billing, dunning
   - Product: inventory, order fulfilment, abandoned-cart flows
2. **Where work happens.** Options: at the customer's site (field/mobile); at our location (storefront, studio, clinic); online/remote; or a mix. Field work brings in dispatch, routing, photo/job notes and mobile invoicing.
3. **Customer type.** Options: consumers (B2C), businesses (B2B), government, or donors and members (nonprofit). B2B brings in proposals, contracts, net-terms invoicing and CRM pipelines. B2C brings in reviews, SMS and booking.
4. **Team size.** Options: just me (nonemployer); 2–4; 5–9; 10–19; 20–49; 50–249. Use bands like these that map to the Census, Pew and UK bands.
5. **Annual revenue band** (optional). Suggested: under $50K; $50–250K; $250K–1M; $1–5M; over $5M.
6. **Regulated data.** Health information (HIPAA), client funds or trust accounting (legal, real estate), payment card data, or minors (childcare, tutoring). This sets privacy guardrails on recommended tools.
7. **Current tool stack and digital maturity.** Options: paper/spreadsheets; basic tools (email, calendar, QuickBooks); vertical software (for example Jobber, Mindbody, Toast); or integrated. This decides whether a plan starts from zero or connects tools the business already uses.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative dataset that quantifies the mix of appointment, project, or subscription models across small businesses. The trait map in the taxonomy below is expert judgment.

## 4. Digital maturity and admin burden by segment

### Takeaway
AI and technology adoption is highest in information, finance/insurance, professional/technical services, and tech and media firms. It is lowest in agriculture, accommodation and food services, construction, and retail, along with small hands-on trades.
- Census BTOS (secondary readings, Dec 2025–May 2026): about 18–20% of firms use AI nationally.
  - Information: about 38–40%
  - Finance: about 34%
  - Professional services: about 33%
  - Health care: about 20%
  - Retail: about 14%
  - Manufacturing: about 12%
  - Accommodation/food: about 8%
  - Agriculture: about 5%
- Self-reported generative-AI use in surveys runs much higher: the US Chamber reports 58% in 2025.

Owner admin time is commonly reported at about 8–16 hours a week, but most of these numbers come from weak, vendor-originated sources.

### Cited Findings
- Fed note (Apr 2026): about 18% of firms had adopted AI by year-end 2025 (Census data). Professional services and financial sectors stand out — [Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/monitoring-ai-adoption-in-the-u-s-economy-20260403.html)
- BTOS by sector, from secondary compilations (verify against Census BTOS):
  - Information 39.7% and Finance and Insurance 33.9% (as of May 3, 2026), against a national rate of about 19.8% — [allfacts.ai](https://allfacts.ai/facts/census-btos-industry-ai-use-may-2026)
  - Range from Information 37.6% down to Agriculture 5.1% (Nov 2025–Feb 2026)
  - Professional services about 33%, Health care about 20%, Manufacturing about 12%, Retail about 14%, Accommodation and food services about 8%

  Sources: [Axis Intelligence](https://axis-intelligence.com/ai-adoption-by-industry-statistics/); [jobsdata.ai](https://jobsdata.ai/predictions/ai-adoption-rate). The Census working paper with the primary detail is [CES-WP-26-25](https://www2.census.gov/library/working-papers/2026/adrm/ces/CES-WP-26-25.pdf)
- 2018 ABS: under 6% of firms used the AI technologies measured, and adoption varied widely by industry — [SSTI summary](https://ssti.org/node/87365); [Census blog](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/blogs/research-matters/2024/12/ai-use-small-businesses.html?tp=1)
- US Chamber "Empowering Small Business" 2025 (3,870 firms under 250 employees, June 2025): 58% use generative AI, up from 40% in 2024 and 23% in 2023. By sector: tech 77%, financial services 74%, entertainment/media 65%, agriculture 58%, construction 47% — [IPWatchdog summary](https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/08/18/us-chamber-report-small-businesses-rapidly-adopting-ai-despite-regulatory-concerns/); [US Chamber](https://www.uschamber.com/technology/empowering-small-business-the-impact-of-technology-on-u-s-small-business). The US Chamber sample is self-selected, and its "use AI" bar is much lower than BTOS's "used AI to produce goods/services". This is why the two levels differ so much.
- Ireland (Small Firms Association, Aug 2026): AI uptake is highest in financial and professional services (51%) and least common in retail, hospitality and manufacturing — [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2026/08/10/fewer-than-one-in-five-small-firms-have-built-ai-into-their-business-models-survey-finds/)
- Restaurants (7shifts 2025, 500+ respondents): 65% adopted a new tool in the past year, but only 49% describe their systems as connected and only 1 in 7 reached the most advanced tech stage. This is a sign of tool sprawl and integration needs — [Eatery Club summary of 7shifts](https://www.eatery.club/en/blog/a-new-report-highlights-the-restaurant-industry-s-digital-divide)
- Construction: the AXA XL Technology Adoption Maturity Index rose from 4.9 (2019) to 6.8 (2024) and was 6.4 in 2025. Respondents are participating contractors, not small firms specifically — [IRMI](https://www.irmi.com/articles/expert-commentary/constructions-digital-maturity-grows-after-years-of-technology-adoption)
- UK (Oct 2025): close to half of respondents in construction, hospitality, manufacturing and healthcare ranked digitalisation as a strong priority. The source notes these sectors are associated with manual processes — [becertified SME Digitalisation Report](https://becertified.com/news/the-sme-digitalisation-report/)
- Admin burden:
  - About 16 hours/week (36% of the week) on admin in a 2023 survey of 251 US entrepreneurs. This is the commonly repeated figure, and its attribution is inconsistent — [ASA blog](https://blog.asa.team/how-much-time-do-employees-spend-on-administrative-tasks/amp/)
  - US microbusinesses (2024, n=837): admin plus legal/compliance took 22.4% of owner time — same source
  - UK (Amex, n=1,000): about 11 hrs/week on admin/finance, roughly twice the time spent on sales — [Asian Trader](https://www.asiantrader.biz/small-business-owners-admin-burden)
  - Australia: 8–12 hrs/week on finance and admin — [ScaleSuite](https://www.scalesuite.com.au/resources/where-sme-owners-spend-their-time)

  All of these are secondary or vendor sources of low-to-moderate reliability.

### Inferences
- **Least digitally mature, highest upside, needs the simplest plans:** food service and hospitality, agriculture, construction and trades, small retail, personal care, auto repair. For these, the intake should assume paper or texting workflows and recommend phone- and SMS-first automations.
- **Most mature:** professional services, finance and insurance, agencies and IT, real estate. Their plans can assume existing SaaS and focus on integration (Zapier/Make, CRM, document automation).
- **Highest admin burden by workflow, inferred from regulation and paperwork intensity:** healthcare and dental (insurance claims, intake forms, HIPAA), childcare (licensing, attendance, parent billing), construction and trades (estimates, permits, change orders, lien waivers, certified payroll), legal and accounting (time tracking, compliance deadlines), trucking (ELD, IFTA, compliance), and nonprofits (donor receipts, grant reporting). None of the retrieved sources ranks admin burden by industry, so this is judgment.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable US source that quantifies admin hours by industry.
- Primary BTOS sector tables could not be retrieved, and the sector percentages above come from secondary compilations with mixed reference periods.
- I did not retrieve the full NFIB 2025 tech survey industry tables, the full US Chamber sector table, Intuit QuickBooks, Salesforce SMB Trends, Verizon, Goldman Sachs 10KSB, or OECD SME digitalization data.

## 5. Proposed 2-level taxonomy for the intake form

### Takeaway
The proposal has 18 top-level categories with 4–9 sub-types each. Each sub-type has default operating-model traits, so the form can pre-fill branching answers and the user can then confirm or override them.

Trait codes:
- Model: **A** = appointment, **W** = walk-in/order, **P** = project/quote, **R** = recurring/subscription, **S** = product sales
- Location: **F** = field/mobile, **L** = fixed location, **O** = online
- Customer: **C** = B2C, **B** = B2B
- **Reg** = regulated data

### Cited Findings
- The taxonomy is grounded in the sector counts above:
  - Professional services is the largest nonemployer sector
  - Transportation is second; real estate is third
  - Construction is about 9.6% of nonemployers and has about 719K small employer firms

  Sources: [Census story](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2025/05/smallest-businesses.html); [Review42](https://resources.review42.com/small-business-statistics/)
- The NAICS sector codes in the "NAICS anchor" column follow the SUSB/NES sector structure — [Census SUSB](https://www.census.gov/data/tables/2022/econ/susb/2022-susb-annual.html)

Form-design notes:
- **Two-question entry:** (1) "What best describes your business?" using the 18–20 tiles; (2) "Which type?" using the sub-type list plus "Other: ___". Pre-fill the trait defaults, then ask the user to confirm: How do customers buy? Where do you work? Who are your customers? Team size? Revenue?
- **Allow multi-select or a "primary + secondary"** for hybrids, such as a bakery with e-commerce or a salon with retail product.
- **Merge or trim the list** if a shorter list is needed. Candidates:
  - Merge 2 into 1 (Trades & Construction)
  - Merge 12 into 11
  - Merge 20 into 7
  - Move 18 into others

  This gives about 15 categories. Keep Beauty, Health, and Food separate because their automation stacks (booking/no-shows, HIPAA intake, POS/menus) differ sharply.
- **Avoid giving gig drivers a dedicated tile.** Despite their large nonemployer count, they have limited automation needs; the Transportation tile covers them.
- **Use traits for maturity-aware branching.** Flag lower-maturity verticals (Food, Retail, Trades, Auto, Agriculture, Personal care) for "start simple" plans, and higher-maturity verticals (Professional, Creative/Tech, Real estate, Finance) for "integrate and orchestrate" plans. This follows the BTOS and US Chamber sector patterns above.

### Gaps
- The taxonomy has not been validated against user research or competitor intake forms (for example Jobber, Square, HubSpot, or QuickBooks onboarding industry pickers). I did not retrieve those.
- Population shares per top-level tile could not be computed without SUSB and NES 6-digit NAICS tables. If the product wants to order tiles by prevalence, the next step is to pull them from data.census.gov.
